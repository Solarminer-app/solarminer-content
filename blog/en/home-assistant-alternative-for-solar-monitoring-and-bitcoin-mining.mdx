If you already monitor your home energy, you may have the beginnings of a useful solar dataset. Adding mining introduces another layer: compatible hardware, a pool, power control and a separate set of costs.

SolarMiner brings energy monitoring and control of compatible mining devices into one application. It runs on your own host. That can suit a focused solar-and-mining use case, but the choice starts with your actual devices and goals.

## Start with the data you need

Solar generation is only one part of the picture. Household consumption, import, export and battery operation affect how much surplus is available at a particular moment.

Check whether your equipment provides the required measurements through an interface that your chosen adapter can read. Manufacturer names alone are not enough. Model, firmware and enabled access matter.

The [hardware directory](/en/supported-inverters) lists recorded devices and interfaces. A listing is not a guarantee of a tested adapter for every model variant.

## Keep power and energy separate

**kW** measures power at a moment in time. **kWh** measures an amount of energy over a period. A brief power reading cannot establish how much energy a miner will consume over a month.

When evaluating a setup, look at real measurements over time. Solar surplus changes with weather, household use and battery operation. Device limits and delayed measurements also affect control.

## Mining hardware is an additional requirement

Software alone does not turn electricity into Bitcoin. You need compatible mining hardware, pool access and suitable energy measurements.

Heat, noise, space and the suitability of your installation are part of that setup. Check device documentation and control capabilities before connecting a miner. A software description cannot guarantee immediate response or completely eliminate grid consumption.

## Compare revenue before comparing profit

The [Bitcoin calculator](/en/pv-mining-calculator) compares estimated mining revenue per kWh with an effective feed-in tariff. It uses Bitcoin price, difficulty, block reward and miner efficiency.

The calculation includes a **2.5% developer fee** as the existing display assumption. Check the actual node configuration separately. Hardware, hosting, maintenance, pool and miner software fees and grid electricity are not deducted.

If mining exceeds the feed-in tariff in that model, the difference is still not your total profit. Actual surplus operating hours and the cost of additional hardware remain part of your own evaluation.

## Use scenarios to understand dependencies

You can change the price, efficiency and feed-in assumptions in the calculator. Its chart varies Bitcoin price while keeping difficulty, efficiency and the tariff fixed.

That is a scenario, not a prediction. Those other factors can change too. If market data is unavailable, the calculator shows an unavailable state instead of quietly using historical fallback values.

## Read benchmarks in context

[Public benchmarks](/en/benchmarks) can help you understand observed hashrate and power consumption. Compare the same algorithm and data source, and check the number of samples.

SolarMiner observations and external reference measurements remain separate. Manufacturer TDP is reference information, not measured mining power. A result for another device or configuration does not guarantee your own outcome.

## Your existing smart-home setup

If you already use Home Assistant or another monitoring system, list the workflows you need before evaluating a change. For SolarMiner, check the documented energy interfaces, supported miner controls and hosting requirements.

The [SolarMiner and Home Assistant page](/en/home-assistant-alternative) frames this as a use-case decision. Current setup information belongs in the [official documentation](https://docs.solarminer.app/setup).

## A clearer starting point

Begin with the hardware directory, test your own assumptions in the calculator and review the setup guide. Follow real energy measurements once the system is configured.

The useful outcome is a transparent view of your options. No tool description or momentary price comparison can promise a specific financial result.
