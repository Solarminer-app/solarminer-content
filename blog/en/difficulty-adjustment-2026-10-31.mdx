Bitcoin's network difficulty has risen sharply: from about **140.6 T** to **148.4 T**, an increase of **5.54%**. Difficulty adjustments happen automatically roughly every two weeks, whenever the total computing power of the network has changed. All figures below come from a snapshot taken on 30 October 2026 and are not financial or investment advice.

## The adjustment in numbers

- Difficulty before: about **140.6 T**
- Difficulty after: about **148.4 T** (**+5.54%**)
- Bitcoin price: about **€69,323**
- Block reward: **3.1485 BTC**

## What it means for surplus mining

For a reference miner (Antminer S19k Pro, 22.13 J/TH), the snapshot yields mining revenue of about **€0.0557 per kWh**. That is slightly below typical grid electricity tariffs of around €0.06–0.08 per kWh, so mining with purchased grid power barely pays off at these levels. The picture differs for solar surplus, whose marginal cost is close to zero: there, mining often remains the most economical way to use electricity that cannot be fed into the grid, even after a difficulty increase. A higher difficulty simply means more computing power is competing in the network, so the revenue per kilowatt-hour falls for every operator.
